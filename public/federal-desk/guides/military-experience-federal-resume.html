Turn military experience into evidence for a federal résumé

Show a civilian reader what you did, who relied on it, and how it meets the vacancy’s specialized experience requirement.

Start with a civilian reader’s question

Find the Qualifications section for your target grade. Suppose a vacancy asks for experience reconciling inventory records and briefing supervisors on discrepancies. A duty title alone gives the reviewer little evidence of either action. Keep your real title, spell out an unfamiliar abbreviation at first use and describe what you personally checked or decided.

Translate the work one decision at a time

A fictional supply specialist starts with “Responsible for equipment accountability.” Their notes specify that they compared laptop checkout records with serial numbers, traced unmatched returns to handoffs and briefed the section lead on the corrections. These details show the work even to someone who has never served in the unit.

Put the evidence under the role where it happened

An illustrative bullet reads, “Reconciled checkout records for 68 training laptops against a physical inventory. Traced five unmatched returns to missing handoff entries, corrected the register and briefed the section lead on a weekly check.” All numbers in this example are fictional. Replace them with facts you can verify. When you lack a defensible number, use the decision, finding or documented output.

Keep a separate list of supporting documents

Record the role’s month-and-year dates and weekly hours. Read the announcement’s Required Documents section for any evidence of eligibility or veterans’ preference. The document list and the résumé serve different purposes. Keep classified, sensitive and personally identifying details out of your work examples.
